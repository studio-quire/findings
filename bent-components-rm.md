# The non-bent components of a quadratic function form a Reed–Muller codeword

*Bent components · quadratic functions · 2026-11-11 · answers an open problem*

## In plain words

> A component of a vectorial function is "bent" when it is as far from linear as a function can be. For quadratic functions, whether a component is bent is decided by one polynomial of low degree, so the components that are not bent always form a set of a very particular kind: the support of a word in a Reed–Muller code. Results from 1969 and 1970 on which sizes such sets can have then say how few non-bent components a function can have. That settles a question a 2026 paper left open (no function meets its bound), improves the paper's bound, and rules out by hand three cases in dimension 8 that the [companion note](apn8-not-sixteen.md) settled by long computer searches. A 1976 description of somewhat heavier codewords raises the bound again from dimension 10 up.

*First version 2026-11-11; the $`(8,6)`$ example showing the bound is attained for $`n=8`$, and the paragraph on how tight Theorem 4 is, added later that day. Theorem 5 added 2026-11-12, Theorem 6 that evening.*

## Setting

Let $`n`$ be even and $`F:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2^m`$ quadratic. For $`b\in\mathbb F_2^m`$ the component $`b\cdot F`$ has an alternating matrix $`M_b=\sum_k b_kM_k`$, where $`M_k`$ belongs to the $`k`$-th coordinate of $`F`$. Let $`N_F=\{b\neq0: b\cdot F \text{ not bent}\}`$ (affine components count as not bent), $`r=n/2`$, and $`\mathrm{RM}(r,m)`$ the Reed–Muller code of Boolean functions on $`\mathbb F_2^m`$ of degree at most $`r`$, with minimum distance $`d=2^{m-r}`$. $`F`$ is *MNBC* (maximum number of bent components) when $`|N_F|=2^{m-r}-1`$, the least possible [1, after Thm III.4].

**Lemma 1** (known [2]). A quadratic Boolean function is bent exactly when its alternating form is nondegenerate, so $`g(b):=[\,b\cdot F\text{ bent}\,]=\det M_b=\mathrm{Pf}(M_b)^2=\mathrm{Pf}(M_b)`$ over $`\mathbb F_2`$. The Pfaffian has degree $`n/2`$ in the entries of $`M_b`$, which are linear in $`b`$, so $`\deg g\le n/2`$, and $`g(0)=0`$.

Carlet and Thornburgh give this formula [2, Remark 6.2], and derive $`\deg g\le n/2`$ for every plateaued $`F`$ from [1, Prop III.1], the fact that a subspace of dimension above $`n/2`$ holds an odd number of non-bent components [2, Lemma 5.3]; the two statements are equivalent. What follows adds one step: the low-weight structure of Reed–Muller codes.

## The bounds

> **Theorem 2.** Let $`n\ge4`$ be even, $`m\ge n/2+1`$, and $`F:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2^m`$ quadratic. Either $`F`$ is MNBC ($`N_F\cup\{0\}`$ is a subspace of dimension $`m-n/2`$), or $`|N_F|\ge 3\cdot2^{m-n/2-1}-1`$.

*Proof.* $`h:=1+g`$ lies in $`\mathrm{RM}(r,m)`$, $`h(0)=1`$, and its weight is $`|N_F|+1`$, since the zeros of $`g`$ are $`0`$ and $`N_F`$. For $`r\ge2`$, a codeword of $`\mathrm{RM}(r,m)`$ whose weight lies in $`[d,2d)`$ has weight $`2d-2^i`$ for some $`i`$ (Berlekamp and Sloane [3]; Kasami and Tokura [4] found exactly which $`i`$ occur and which codewords have them; see [5, Ch. 15, Thm 11] and [6, Thm 2]). A weight above $`d`$ of that form is at least $`2d-2^{m-r-1}=1.5d`$. Codewords of weight $`d`$ are the indicators of $`(m-r)`$-dimensional affine flats [5, Ch. 13]; as $`h(0)=1`$, the flat is a linear subspace and $`F`$ is MNBC. Otherwise $`|N_F|+1\ge1.5d=3\cdot2^{m-r-1}`$. ∎

The theorem says more than parity only from $`m\ge n/2+3`$: $`|N_F|`$ is odd [1, Prop III.1], which already gives the bound for $`m=n/2+1`$ and $`m=n/2+2`$. From $`m=n/2+3`$ on it is new. Bénéteau, Goluboff, Kölsch and Vaghasiya [1, Thm III.7] proved, for $`n\le 2m-6`$ (that is, $`m\ge n/2+3`$), that a non-MNBC $`F`$ has $`|N_F|\ge 2^{m-r}+2^{m-r-2}+2^{m-r-3}-1=11\cdot2^{m-r-3}-1`$; Theorem 2 gives $`12\cdot2^{m-r-3}-1`$.

> **Corollary 3** (answers [1, Open Problem VI.4]: no). For even $`n\le 2m-6`$, no quadratic $`F:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2^m`$ has exactly $`2^{m-n/2}+2^{m-n/2-2}+2^{m-n/2-3}-1`$ non-trivial non-bent components.

For $`n\ge4`$ this value lies strictly between the MNBC value and Theorem 2's bound. For $`n=2`$, $`h=1+g`$ is affine, so $`|N_F|`$ is $`2^{m-1}-1`$ or $`2^m-1`$, and the value is neither.

*A second proof, with the paper's own tools* ($`n\le2m-6`$). Equality makes $`N_F`$ a smallest nontrivial blocking set with respect to $`(n/2)`$-spaces of $`\mathrm{PG}(m-1,2)`$ [1, proof of Thm III.7]. By Govaerts and Storme [7, Thm 1.4(3)] it is then a cone: a vertex $`V`$ of vector dimension $`m-n/2-3`$ and, in a 4-dimensional $`U`$ with $`U\cap V=0`$, the base $`B=l\cup l_1\cup l_2\cup l_3`$, three concurrent lines through a point $`P`$ that span $`U`$ and a line $`l`$ skew to them (the complement of an elliptic quadric in $`\mathrm{PG}(3,2)`$). For $`Q\in l`$ the line $`PQ`$ meets $`B`$ exactly in $`P`$ and $`Q`$. Take $`C`$ complementary to $`V\oplus U`$ (dimension $`n/2-1`$) and $`W=\langle P,Q\rangle\oplus C`$, of dimension $`n/2+1`$. Since $`N_F\cup\{0\}=V+(B\cup\{0\})\subseteq V\oplus U`$ and $`W\cap(V\oplus U)=\langle P,Q\rangle`$, $`W`$ contains exactly two non-bent components, but [1, Prop III.1] says the number is odd. ∎ (The wording of [7, Thm 1.4] used here is that of a 2004 draft of [7], consistent with [1]'s quotation of the published theorem.)

> **Theorem 4.** Let $`n\ge4`$ be even and $`F:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2^n`$ quadratic APN. Then $`|N_F|\ge 2^{n/2+1}-3`$: $`F`$ has at most $`2^n-2^{n/2+1}+2`$ bent components.

*Proof.* $`|N_F|\equiv1\pmod 4`$ [1, Thm III.8; 8, Lemma 6.4]. (Directly: the fourth moment of the Walsh transform makes $`\sum_{b\ne0}2^{k_b}=2(2^n-1)`$, where $`2^{(n+k_b)/2}`$ is the amplitude of $`b\cdot F`$ ($`2^{k_b}`$ is the size of its radical), so $`\sum_{b\in N_F}(2^{k_b}-1)=2^n-1`$; each $`k_b\ge2`$ is even, so each term is $`3`$ modulo $`4`$, as is $`2^n-1`$.) So the weight of $`h`$ is $`\equiv2\pmod4`$. Here $`d=2^{n/2}`$, and if the weight is below $`2d`$ it is $`2d-2^i`$, which is $`\equiv2\pmod4`$ only for $`i=1`$: weight $`2^{n/2+1}-2`$. ∎

At equality, $`N_F\cup\{0\}`$ is, by Kasami and Tokura's description of these codewords [4; 9, Thm 2.10], the symmetric difference of two $`(n/2)`$-dimensional affine flats that meet in one point. Equality holds for $`n=4`$: $`x^3`$ on $`\mathbb F_{16}`$ has 5 non-bent components.

**What Theorem 4 adds.** [1] has three lower bounds for quadratic APN functions and compares them [1, Prop IV.4], by the dimension $`k`$ of the largest radical (linearity $`2^{(n+k)/2}`$). For $`k\neq n/2`$ their bounds (A) and (C), or simply counting points ($`k>n/2`$ forces $`|N_F|\ge2^k+1`$), already exceed $`2^{n/2+1}`$. Only for $`k=n/2`$, which needs $`4\mid n`$ since $`k`$ is even, was Thm III.9 their best: $`2^{n/2}+2^{n/2-2}+2^{n/2-3}-1`$, now $`2^{n/2+1}-3`$ (21 → 29 for $`n=8`$, 87 → 125 for $`n=12`$). So for $`4\mid n`$ Theorem 4 improves the lower bound on $`|N_F|`$ over all quadratic APN functions; for $`n\equiv2\pmod4`$ it adds nothing.

Below $`2d`$, weights give no more than that. But the shape of the equality case, with the way the radicals of a quadratic APN function partition the space, rules equality out beyond $`n=4`$.

> **Theorem 5.** Let $`n\ge6`$ be even and $`F:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2^n`$ quadratic APN. Then $`|N_F|\ge2^{n/2+1}+1`$. So Theorem 4 is attained only for $`n=4`$.

*Proof.* Write $`B_v`$ for the alternating form of $`v\cdot F`$ (it is linear in $`v`$), $`R_v`$ for its radical and $`k_v=\dim R_v`$, which is even. Every nonzero $`a\in\mathbb F_2^n`$ lies in $`R_v`$ for exactly one $`v\neq0`$ [1, Thm II.3], so radicals of distinct components meet only in 0 and $`\sum_{v\in N_F}(2^{k_v}-1)=2^n-1`$. By Theorem 4 there are several non-bent components, so none is affine (by the partition it would be the only one) and every $`k_v<n`$. If $`k_v=k`$, the other radicals have dimension at most $`n-k`$ and cover the $`2^n-2^k`$ points off $`R_v`$, so $`|N_F|\ge1+(2^n-2^k)/(2^{n-k}-1)=2^k+1`$.

*$`n\equiv2\pmod 4`$.* The largest $`k_v`$ is even and $`n/2`$ is odd. If it is above $`n/2`$, the count just made gives $`|N_F|\ge2^{n/2+1}+1`$. If it is below, every radical has at most $`2^{n/2-1}-1`$ nonzero points, and $`|N_F|\ge(2^n-1)/(2^{n/2-1}-1)>2^{n/2+1}`$.

*$`n=4t`$, $`t\ge2`$.* As $`|N_F|\equiv1\pmod4`$, it is enough to rule out $`|N_F|=2M-3`$, where $`M=2^{2t}`$: equality in Theorem 4. Suppose it holds.

1. Every $`k_v\le2t`$, since $`2^{k_v}+1\le2M-3`$. Let $`Q`$ be the set of components with $`k_v=2t`$ and $`a=|Q|`$. The other non-bent components have $`k_v\le2t-2`$, so $`a(M-1)+(2M-3-a)(M/4-1)\ge M^2-1`$, which gives $`a\ge2M/3+11/3-16/(3M)`$ ($`a\ge14`$ for $`n=8`$, $`a\ge47`$ for $`n=12`$).
2. The word $`1+g`$ has weight $`2M-2=2d-2`$ in $`\mathrm{RM}(2t,4t)`$. By Kasami and Tokura's forms [4; 9, Thm 2.10] it is affinely equivalent to $`x_1\cdots x_{2t}+x_{2t+1}\cdots x_{4t}`$ (their other form would need $`2t+2\ge4t`$). So $`N_F\cup\{0\}`$ is the symmetric difference of two $`2t`$-dimensional affine flats with complementary directions that meet in one point $`p`$. The one through 0 is a subspace $`W\ni p`$, the other is $`p+U`$ with $`U\oplus W=\mathbb F_2^n`$, and $`N_F=(W\setminus\{0,p\})\cup\big((p+U)\setminus\{p\}\big)`$.
3. Two elements $`c\ne c'`$ of $`Q`$ have radicals that meet in 0, so they are complementary, and then $`c+c'`$ is bent: if $`x`$ is in the radical of $`B_c+B_{c'}`$, the functional $`B_c(x,\cdot)=B_{c'}(x,\cdot)`$ vanishes on $`R_c`$ and on $`R_{c'}`$, hence everywhere, and $`x\in R_c\cap R_{c'}=0`$. Two elements of $`Q`$ in $`W`$ would sum to a bent point of $`W\setminus\{0\}`$, and the only one is $`p`$; so at most two lie in $`W`$. Let $`T=\{u\in U:p+u\in Q\}`$ and $`G=U\setminus T`$, which contains 0: $`|T|\ge a-2`$ and $`|G|\le M+2-a`$.
4. Fix $`u\in T`$, let $`R=R_{p+u}`$ and $`q(x)=\mathrm{Pf}(B_x|_R)`$ for $`x\in U`$. The Pfaffian of a $`2t\times2t`$ alternating matrix has degree $`t`$ in its entries, so $`q`$ is a word of $`\mathrm{RM}(t,2t)`$ on $`U`$, with $`q(0)=0`$. As $`B_{p+u}`$ vanishes on $`R`$, $`B_p|_R=B_u|_R`$, so for $`u'\in T\setminus\{u\}`$ the form $`B_{u+u'}|_R=B_{p+u'}|_R`$ is nondegenerate ($`B_{p+u'}`$ comes from a nondegenerate form on $`\mathbb F_2^n/R_{p+u'}`$, onto which $`R`$ maps isomorphically): $`q(u+u')=1`$. So $`z(x)=1+q(x+u)`$ is a word of $`\mathrm{RM}(t,2t)`$ with $`z(u)=1`$ that vanishes on $`T\setminus\{u\}`$.
5. If $`f\in\mathrm{RM}(t-1,2t)`$ vanishes on $`G`$, then $`f(u)=\sum_xf(x)z(x)=0`$, since $`z`$ is supported on $`G\cup\{u\}`$ and $`fz`$ has degree below $`2t`$. So $`f`$ vanishes on $`G\cup T=U`$: restriction to $`G`$ is injective on $`\mathrm{RM}(t-1,2t)`$, and $`|G|\ge\dim\mathrm{RM}(t-1,2t)=\big(M-\binom{2t}{t}\big)/2`$.
6. With 1 and 3 this needs $`\binom{2t}{t}\ge M/3+10/3-32/(3M)`$, which fails: $`6<8`$ for $`t=2`$ (there $`|G|\le4`$, and $`\dim\mathrm{RM}(1,4)=5`$), $`20<24.5`$ for $`t=3`$, and $`\binom{2t}{t}/4^t`$ decreases, so for $`t\ge3`$ it is at most $`20/64<1/3`$. ∎

Steps 4 and 5 use nothing about $`p`$: in a quadratic APN function on $`\mathbb F_2^{4t}`$, a $`2t`$-dimensional affine flat of components holds at most $`\big(2^{2t}+\binom{2t}{t}\big)/2`$ components whose radical has dimension $`2t`$ (11 for $`n=8`$).

Above $`2d`$, Kasami, Tokura and Azumi determined every weight of a Reed–Muller code below $`2.5d`$ [10] (their paper states the results; the proofs are in their 1974 report). For $`n\ge10`$ that range again leaves few values. Theorem 6 rests on their determination.

> **Theorem 6.** Let $`n=2r\ge10`$, $`M=2^r`$, and $`F:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2^n`$ quadratic APN. Then $`|N_F|`$ is $`\tfrac52M-7`$ or $`\tfrac52M-3`$, or at least $`\tfrac52M+1`$. In particular $`|N_F|\ge5\cdot2^{n/2-1}-7`$: at least 73, 153, 313 and 633 for $`n=10,12,14,16`$. This is new for $`n\ge12`$; for $`n=10`$ it is a second proof of 73 (below).

*Proof.* The weight $`w=|N_F|+1`$ of $`h=1+g\in\mathrm{RM}(r,2r)`$ is $`\equiv2\pmod4`$, and by Theorems 4 and 5 it is at least $`2d`$, where $`d=M`$. Kasami, Tokura and Azumi list all weights of $`\mathrm{RM}(r,m)`$ in $`[2d,2.5d)`$ [10, pp. 392–394]. Written in units of $`d`$ in binary they are $`10`$, $`10.001`$, $`10.01`$, $`10.0101`$, $`10.011`$, $`10.0111`$; $`10.0\underbrace{1\cdots1}_{k}`$ for $`4\le k\le m-r-\lceil m/r\rceil`$; and $`10.0\underbrace{1\cdots1}_{k}01`$ for $`2\le k\le m-r-\lceil m/r\rceil-2`$. Here $`m=2r`$, so $`\lceil m/r\rceil=2`$, and $`d=2^r\ge32`$. Only two of these weights are $`\equiv2\pmod4`$: $`\tfrac52d-6`$ (which is $`10.0101\,d`$ when $`d=32`$, and otherwise the last family with $`k=r-4`$) and $`\tfrac52d-2`$ ($`10.0111\,d`$ when $`d=32`$, otherwise the one before with $`k=r-2`$). So $`w`$ is one of them, or $`w\ge\tfrac52d`$; then $`|N_F|\ge\tfrac52d-1`$, and as $`|N_F|\equiv1\pmod4`$, $`|N_F|\ge\tfrac52d+1`$. ∎

At the two low values the set is explicit. There $`h`$ vanishes on no affine hyperplane: otherwise its weight would be that of a word of $`\mathrm{RM}(r-1,2r-1)`$ (its restriction to the complementary hyperplane), which is divisible by 4 by McEliece's theorem [5, Ch. 15]. And $`h`$ depends on all $`2r`$ coordinates: otherwise its weight is $`2^{2r-j}`$ times the weight of a function of degree at most $`r`$ in $`j<2r`$ variables, a multiple of 4 when $`j\le2r-2`$ and twice an even number when $`j=2r-1>r`$. So [10, Thm 2] applies: $`h`$ is affinely equivalent to one of the words of their Table I in exactly $`2r`$ variables, and of those with degree $`r`$ only two have weight $`\equiv2\pmod 4`$ (check 8), apart from one more for $`n=10`$. For $`|N_F|=\tfrac52M-7`$, $`h`$ is affinely equivalent to

```math
x_1\cdots x_r+x_{r+1}\cdots x_{2r-2}\,\big(x_{2r-1}x_{2r}+x_{r-1}x_r\big),
```

so $`N_F\cup\{0\}`$ is $`P\,\triangle\,S`$, where $`P`$ is an affine $`r`$-flat and $`S`$ is the set of $`\tfrac32M`$ points of an affine $`(r+2)`$-flat at which a quadratic form of rank 4 is 1, with $`|P\cap S|=3`$. For $`|N_F|=\tfrac52M-3`$ the same holds with $`x_{r-1}(x_r+1)`$ in place of $`x_{r-1}x_r`$ and $`|P\cap S|=1`$, or, for $`n=10`$ only, $`h`$ is in a second class, their Table I's (18).

**What Theorem 6 adds.** For every even $`n\ge12`$ it raises the lower bound on $`|N_F|`$ over all quadratic APN functions. When $`4\mid n`$ the case it improves is a largest radical of dimension $`n/2`$, and the gain over Theorem 5 is $`2^{n/2-1}-8`$ (129 → 153 for $`n=12`$). When $`n\equiv2\pmod4`$ the cases are largest radicals of dimension $`n/2\pm1`$, where counting, [1]'s bounds and [2, Cor 5.13] give about $`2^{n/2+1}+n`$ (273 → 313 for $`n=14`$). For $`n=10`$ it gives, without [1]'s list of distributions, the 73 that the list gives with [2, Cor 5.13]; the two smallest values are the distributions $`[0^{702+4i},2^{320-5i},4^i,6^1]`$ with $`i=62`$ and $`i=61`$. Weights alone give no more than this if, as Carlet and Solé conjecture [6], every even weight from $`2.5d`$ to $`2^{2r}-2.5d`$ occurs in $`\mathrm{RM}(r,2r)`$; that is proved for $`r\le5`$ [6, 11]. Going further means ruling out the shapes above.

**Dimension 8.** The distributions $`[0^{238-4i},2^{5i},4^{17-i}]`$ of [1, Thm IV.5] have $`|N_F|=4i+17`$, so Theorem 4 leaves $`i\ge3`$ and Theorem 5 leaves $`i\ge4`$; the remaining type $`[0^{190},2^{64},6^1]`$ has no rank-4 components. So no quadratic APN function on $`\mathbb F_2^8`$ has more than 13 rank-4 components. That covers the $`i=1`$ case of [1, Open Problem VI.1] (first settled by Carlet and Thornburgh [2, Cor 5.13]) and the cases $`i=2`$ and $`i=3`$ (until now settled only by the searches in the [companion note](apn8-not-sixteen.md)), without a computer. It stops there: for $`i=4`$ the word $`1+g`$ has weight $`34\ge2d`$, beyond the range of [4], and Theorem 6's argument needs $`n\ge10`$ (every even weight from 28 to 228 occurs in $`\mathrm{RM}(4,8)`$ [6]). What [10] does give for $`i=4`$ is the shape. The number of words of weight 34 in $`\mathrm{RM}(4,8)`$, from the code's weight distribution [10, Table IV] (found independently by Sarwate and Berlekamp and by van Tilborg, as [10] notes) and from formula (b), equals the size of the affine orbit of $`x_1x_2x_3x_4+x_5x_6(x_7x_8+x_3x_4)`$, the case $`r=4`$ of the word above (check 8), so all of them are affinely equivalent and $`N_F\cup\{0\}`$ is, up to an affine map, this one set of 34 points. We have not ruled it out by hand: the condition of step 3 (pairwise sums of the 13 rank-4 components are bent) and the Pfaffians of step 4, one component at a time, leave room, so it would take the radicals themselves, as the searches did. An argument that rules out this shape for every $`r`$ would settle $`i=4`$ and raise Theorem 6 by 4.

## More from the same argument

- **All plateaued functions.** Lemma 1 holds for every plateaued $`F`$ with $`n`$ even [2, Lemma 5.3], and the proofs above use nothing else, so Theorem 2 holds for plateaued $`F`$ and Theorem 4 for plateaued APN $`F`$ (the mod-4 argument above works for them unchanged). Theorem 5 uses the alternating forms, so for plateaued APN $`F`$ with $`n\ge10`$ Theorem 6's argument leaves $`2M-3`$ as a possible value besides its list.
- **The whole low range.** If $`|N_F|<2^{m-n/2+1}-1`$, then $`|N_F|=2^{m-n/2+1}-2^i-1`$ for an $`i`$ allowed by [4] (see [6, Thm 2]). More generally, if $`|N_F|+1<5\cdot2^{m-n/2-1}`$, then $`|N_F|+1`$ is a weight of $`\mathrm{RM}(n/2,m)`$ below $`2.5d`$, and [4] and [10, pp. 392–394] list them all (for plateaued $`F`$ too).
- **Divisibility.** By McEliece's theorem [5, Ch. 15], $`|N_F|+1`$ is divisible by $`2^{\lceil 2m/n\rceil-1}`$, which says more than parity once $`m>n`$.

## How tight

For $`n=4`$, Theorem 2 is the true minimum: over all subspaces of $`\mathrm{Alt}(\mathbb F_2^4)`$, the smallest non-MNBC values of $`|N_F|`$ for $`m=4,\dots,7`$ are 5, 11, 23 and 47, exactly the bound ([1]'s gives 10, 21, 43 for $`m=5,6,7`$). Appending a zero coordinate doubles both $`|N_F|+1`$ and $`d`$, so a function on the bound stays on it: $`x^3`$ on $`\mathbb F_{16}`$ gives tightness for $`n=4`$ and every $`m\ge4`$, and a quadratic $`(6,5)`$-function with 5 non-bent components (found by search; it is in the code folder) for $`n=6`$ and every $`m\ge5`$.

For $`n=8`$ and every $`m\ge6`$, take $`\alpha`$ primitive in $`\mathbb F_{16}`$ and

```math
F:\mathbb F_{16}^2\to\mathbb F_{16}\times\mathbb F_2^2,\qquad F(x,y)=\big(xy,\ \mathrm{Tr}(\alpha x^3),\ \mathrm{Tr}(\alpha^2y^3)\big).
```

Its components are $`\mathrm{Tr}(axy)+c_1\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha x^3)+c_2\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha^2y^3)`$. For $`c=0`$ and $`a\neq0`$ they are Maiorana–McFarland bent. With exactly one $`c_i=1`$ they are still bent for $`a\ne0`$, since a function of $`x`$ alone (or $`y`$ alone) is added, and not bent for $`a=0`$. With $`c_1=c_2=1`$, the component with $`a=0`$ is bent, a sum of bent functions of $`x`$ and of $`y`$ ($`\alpha`$ and $`\alpha^2`$ are not cubes), and of the others exactly the three with $`a^3=\alpha^3`$ are not bent (by computation). So $`|N_F|=5`$: two components of rank 4 and three of rank 6. In the family $`(xy,q_1(x),q_2(y))`$ with $`q_1,q_2`$ quadratic forms on $`\mathbb F_{16}`$, $`|N_F|=5`$ exactly when, up to affine terms, $`q_1=\mathrm{Tr}(\lambda x^3)`$ and $`q_2=\mathrm{Tr}(\mu y^3)`$ with $`\lambda,\mu`$ not cubes and $`\lambda\mu`$ a cube (50 of the $`64^2`$ pairs of alternating forms). Whether Theorem 2 is tight for $`n\ge10`$ is open (it would take an $`(n,n/2+2)`$-function with exactly 5 non-bent components).

Theorem 4 is attained only for $`n=4`$ (Theorem 5). Theorem 5 is attained only for $`n=6`$: a quadratic APN function with distribution $`[0^{46},2^{16},4^1]`$ exists [1, Sec II.C], from the classification in dimension 6, and has $`|N_F|=17`$; for $`n=8`$ the searches of the [companion note](apn8-not-sixteen.md) give $`|N_F|\ge65`$, against 33, and for $`n\ge10`$ Theorem 6 is larger. Whether Theorem 6 is attained for some $`n`$ is open.

## Checks

`checks.py` in [code/bent-components-rm](code/bent-components-rm), a few minutes in all: the degree of $`g`$ on Gold functions in dimensions 6, 8 and 10 and on random quadratic $`(n,m)`$-functions (never above $`n/2`$); Kasami and Tokura's weights against an exhaustive enumeration of $`\mathrm{RM}(2,5)`$ and $`\mathrm{RM}(2,6)`$; the table for $`n=4`$; the $`(6,5)`$ example with its paddings; and the $`(8,6)`$ example with its paddings, which of its components are not bent, and the scan of its family; for Theorem 5, the inequality of its steps 1 and 6 for $`n=8,12,\dots,48`$, the condition of step 4 for $`n=8`$ by brute force over every set $`T`$ and every word of $`\mathrm{RM}(2,4)`$, and, given a file of 8-bit APN functions, the radical partition and the two facts of steps 3 and 4 on every pair of rank-4 components (1,878 pairs in the 517 known functions with two to four of them that we tried); for Theorem 6, which weights of Kasami, Tokura and Azumi's list are $`\equiv2\pmod4`$ for $`m=2r`$, $`r=5,\dots,12`$, every entry of their Table I of degree $`r`$ evaluated directly for $`r=5,6,7`$ (each has the weight the table states), and the affine stabilizer of $`x_1x_2x_3x_4+x_5x_6(x_7x_8+x_3x_4)`$, of order $`2^{13}\cdot3^4=663{,}552`$, whose orbit has exactly their number of weight-34 words of $`\mathrm{RM}(4,8)`$. Before publishing, the argument was reviewed by Claude Fable 5.2, a different model from the author, which re-derived each step, checked the second proof's base on coordinates, found an independent $`(6,5)`$ example, and corrected the comparison for Theorem 4 and the attribution of Lemma 1. The $`(8,6)`$ example and the paragraph on Theorem 4's tightness were added later the same day and reviewed separately by Claude Fable 5.2, which confirmed the example, its paddings and the family scan with code of its own and narrowed the open range for Theorem 4. Theorem 5 (2026-11-12) was reviewed the same way before it went up: Claude Fable 5.2 re-derived each step, checked the facts of steps 3 and 4 on the 521 known functions with code of its own, found that the page had cited the weights-only statement [6] for the shape at equality, and gave the direct argument for $`n\equiv2\pmod4`$. Theorem 6 (the same evening) was reviewed the same way: Claude Fable 5.2 read Kasami, Tokura and Azumi's list and Table I from the page images and re-derived the parity argument, the shapes and the dimension-8 count, which it matched to the weight distribution in their Table IV, recomputed the stabilizer with code of its own, and corrected the comparison for $`n\equiv2\pmod4`$, where [2, Cor 5.13] had already raised the earlier bound.
